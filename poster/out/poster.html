Background & questions

  • Leading cause of death; ~50% of CAD first presents as a heart attack.
  • Clinical scores omit genetics and imaging; how much imaging and genetic risk overlap was unknown.
  1. Which imaging modalities add to clinical, metabolic, ECG and genetic factors?
  2. Overlap between imaging-derived and polygenic risk
  3. Sequential integration: discrimination, calibration, stratification

Cardiac MRI does not image coronary atherosclerosis; its signal reflects remodeling, vascular stiffness and cardiometabolic burden.

Outcome = clinically recognized CAD (MI, angina, revascularization); prevalent cases excluded. Fig. 1 of the manuscript: three data domains → tabular features → classification and Cox survival models.

Methods in brief

  • Encoders fine-tuned on balanced case–control training data only.
  • Embeddings → PCA, 10 PCs per stream (80 total).
  • PRS-CS, European meta-GWAS: OR 1.87 per SD, AUC 0.766 (with age, sex, 20 PCs).
  • XGBoost + nested Cox; imaging risk score = cross-fitted ridge Cox.
IMAGING REPRESENTATIONS
Deep embeddings beat handcrafted cardiac phenotypes
0.770 vs 0.666 AUC, deep embeddings vs conventional imaging-derived phenotypes (IDPs); both combined 0.775

Embeddings outperform IDPs; adding IDPs barely helps (logistic models; performance plateaus at ~5 PCs per modality; Fig. 2a,b).

Embeddings encode physiology: CV1 loads on stroke volume, ejection fraction, myocardial mass and wall thickness (Fig. 2c).

Medical pretraining wins: Med3D for the cardiac 3D encoders, RadImageNet for liver, pancreas and DXA (axes from 0.50 / 0.45; Supp. Fig. 3b, 4b).

Sequential tiers enrich risk far beyond clinical stratification (5-yr incidence, held-out set). Leave-one-centre-out: AUC 0.785–0.822, C-index 0.73–0.76; discrimination is stable across the four UK Biobank imaging centres (site-level robustness, not external validation).

Conclusions

  • Imaging and polygenic risk are nearly uncorrelated: complementary axes.
  • Long-axis cine and aortic distensibility MRI carry the incremental signal; blood-flow, liver, pancreas and DXA add little once clinical variables are included.
  • Clinical → PRS → imaging integration isolates a 36.8% 5-yr-risk subgroup.
  • A framework for complementary risk — not yet a clinical screening tool.

Limitations

  • No CAC/CTA in UK Biobank: no calcium-score benchmark.
  • European-ancestry, complete-case, healthier-than-general cohort; covariates measured at imaging, so part of imaging's gain may reflect temporal proximity.
  • Not externally validated; observational — prospective trials needed.
